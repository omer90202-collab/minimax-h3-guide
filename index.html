The core idea

One prompt. Three modalities.

Most video models take a prompt and maybe an image. H3 reads text, images, video and audio as one unified context, and you describe the relationship between them in plain language.

Capabilities

Eight things worth knowing

01 · Omni-reference

9 images, 3 videos, 3 audio clips

Up to 12 files in one request. Give each reference exactly one job — identity, wardrobe, lighting, motion, voice.

02 · Native audio

Stereo generated in the same pass

32 kHz dialogue, SFX and ambience — jointly modelled, not bolted on afterwards. 11 languages are stable.

03 · Multi-shot

Cuts inside a single generation

Native multi-shot modelling means a 15-second clip can contain real edits, not one unbroken take.

04 · Editing

Change a clip by describing it

Swap a character, alter an object, change the sound or re-pace — without re-rolling. This is the task H3 ranks #1 in.

05 · Text & brands

Legible type, real logos

One of H3's genuinely unusual strengths: it renders small text and real brand marks accurately on request.

06 · V2V motion

Transfer camera and motion

Point at a reference clip and H3 lifts its camera move and pacing onto entirely new subjects.

07 · In-context 2K

Regeneration, not upscaling

Instead of a bolt-on super-res module, the base model regenerates its own output while re-reading the original context.

08 · Aspect ratios

21:9 through 9:16

Cinematic scope, square, and vertical for social — all native, no cropping pass required.

How to prompt it

Give every reference exactly one job.

That single rule fixes most bad H3 output.

H3 reads everything as one merged context. The structure below is what stops it averaging your references.

The section order

Stable facts first, then action, then finishing.

[REFERENCE USE]What each file controls — and what it must not bring
[IDENTITY LOCKS]Subject count, faces, wardrobe, prop and voice ownership
[SCENE]Location, what each character wants, the emotional turn
[DIALOGUE]Exact quoted lines, one named speaker each
[SCREEN GEOGRAPHY]Who is frame-left, who is frame-right, matching eyelines
[SHOT LIST]Timed beats — one primary event each
[ACTING]Observable behaviour, never emotion labels
[LIGHT AND IMAGE]Lighting, palette, lens character, depth
[CAMERA]Permitted moves and transitions — one owner only
[PRODUCTION SOUND]Ambience, effects, dialogue clarity, music policy
[NEGATIVES]The specific failures you are guarding against

The rule that matters most

Every reference gets one job — and one exclusion
Image 1 defines Maya's face, hair and wardrobe only.
Do not inherit its background, pose, framing or lighting.

That second line is what actually stops the leakage.

Timing the beats

1/3Establish — who, where, the relationship, the first line
2/3Escalate — closer framing, the interruption, the reaction
3/3Resolve — the answer, the decisive action, camera settles
Worked example · 8 seconds 
Cinematic live-action product video with warm sunrise light
and restrained color contrast.

Image 1 defines the woman's face, copper hair, and green jacket.
Image 2 defines the rooftop garden and sunrise lighting.
Video 1 provides the woman's measured walking pace, slow camera
movement, and the warm vocal timbre and calm delivery heard in its audio.

The woman walks along the rooftop path while holding a black travel cup.
The camera follows her from the front-left with a slow, controlled
tracking movement. After three steps, she raises the cup, looks toward
the camera, and says, "Take the morning with you."
End on a stable close view of her face and the cup.

overall_soundscape: light wind, soft footsteps, fabric movement,
  clear dialogue.
non_diegetic_music: N/A
+Give every reference one job.
+Tie sound to something visible.
+One sentence of dialogue per 5 seconds.
+Start with one subject, one action, one camera move.
